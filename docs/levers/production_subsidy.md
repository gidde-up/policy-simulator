# Lever: production subsidy

A subsidy at rate s on sector j lowers its unit cost: a DomesticCostShock
dc[j] = -s, propagated through the same price-side Leontief model as the
tariff (dp = L_I' dc, all components <= 0 for a subsidy). The price fall
raises demand through two channels, both gains:
- **downstream**: lower producer prices raise demand for the cheaper
  output (own-price compensated elasticity eta, same parameter as the
  tariff downstream channel, sign reversed);
- **real income**: lower consumer prices raise household purchasing
  power across all sectors.

Fiscal cost = s x baseline output of j, carrying the financing drag
(default on). Net employment then follows the intensity-vs-basket rule:
subsidising a labour-intensive sector (jobs-rich per dollar) is
net-positive; subsidising a capital-intensive sector (e.g.
manufacturing) is typically net-negative once the tax-financing drag on
the more labour-intensive household basket is netted off.
